Important examples behind the recommendation

  • PT1, real Portuguese restructuring news: Gemma with reasoning caught the shift from possibility to eventual certainty, rejected both candidates and proposed a separately checked correction. Qwen still selected the MADLAD candidate with that mistake, both with and without reasoning.
  • PT2, real Portuguese financial figures: Gemma with reasoning retained the existing translation. Qwen without reasoning proposed a rewrite even though the main figures and meaning were already preserved.
  • VI2, real Vietnamese projections: both reasoning models corrected invented currencies back to VND/dong and preserved projected profits and no borrowing plans. These corrections were checked by the same model, not independently verified.
  • Gemma’s two reasoning failures were evidence-quote mismatches: an HTML apostrophe was decoded in L3_ru_en, and separate source fragments were joined with an ellipsis in F_VI1. These are validation failures, not proof of a missed semantic error; they remain withheld and unassessed in the metric.
  • Qwen without reasoning and Gemma with reasoning still approved the disputed NLLB covenant wording. The previous labels remain unchanged and provisional.

The next evaluation should simplify response formatting and improve number/date and evidence-span handling while preserving automatic withholding on unresolved cases. It should then use new examples and an independent reviewer before any live correction or scoring integration. Running both models and accepting their agreement is not sufficient evidence of correctness.

qwen / candidate_2: 'Suspenderam' is the formal/legal term 'suspended'; 'paused' is a less formal synonym. In a 45-day context the practical meaning is the same, so this is a wording choice rather than a material error.

qwen / candidate_1: The source states that the suspension does not IMPLY forgiveness (i.e., the suspension is not a form of or does not entail forgiveness of the debt). Candidate B rewrites this as 'the suspension does not forgive the debt,' which changes the subject of the action and the legal claim. The original is a statement about the semantic/legal relationship between suspension and forgiveness; B turns it into a statement about whether the suspension performs the act of forgiving.
